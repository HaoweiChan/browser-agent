Port Meridian (Alderic: Havn Meridian) is the capital and largest city of the Eastern Littoral province of the Republic of Alder, on the western shore of Meridian Bay. With 1,482,317 residents at the 2020 census it is the republic's second-largest city, and its metropolitan area of just under four million is the second-largest conurbation in the country. The city is the republic's principal port and a centre of marine engineering and finance.

Geography

The city occupies the western shore of Meridian Bay, a drowned river valley that opens onto the Alder Sea. The old town sits on a low spit between the bay and the harbour basin; the nineteenth-century grid climbs the slope behind it towards Harrow Point, the headland that shelters the bay from the prevailing north-westerlies and carries the beacon the city is named for.

Demographics

At the 2020 census the city proper recorded 1,482,317 residents, up from 1,391,004 in 2010. The metropolitan area, which takes in the bay's eastern shore and the three river towns upstream, is home to just under four million people and is the second-largest conurbation in the Republic of Alder.

History

A fishing settlement on the spit is recorded from the fourteenth century; the town charter dates from 1611, when the Meridian Light was raised on Harrow Point and the bay became the safe anchorage on an otherwise open coast. The harbour basin was cut in the 1790s, the railway arrived in 1852, and the city more than tripled in the half-century that followed. Much of the old town was rebuilt after the 1911 fire, in the brick-and-tile style the waterfront keeps.

Economy

The port remains the largest employer. Container traffic moved to the outer harbour at Harrow Point in the 1990s, and the inner basin was redeveloped for ferries, fishing and the cruise trade. Financial services, marine engineering and a cluster of instrument makers around the university make up most of the rest of the metropolitan economy.
